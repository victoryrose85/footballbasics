Primary source: the official 2026 Official Playing Rules of the NFL (the PDF linked from NFL Football Operations' rulebook page), read in full text. Rule numbers below are that book's. ## Changes to the drive

One, in lesson 9 (the offensive holding play). The rulebook enforces a hold on a run from the **spot of the foul** when the hold happens behind the end of the run (the three-and-one method, 14-3-6), and from the previous spot only when the hold happens at or behind the line of scrimmage. The spec's numbers (1st & 20 at own 42, which is 10 yards back from the opponent's 48) are right only if the hold happens at the line of scrimmage, such as a guard grabbing a defender at the snap. So lesson 9 now says the hold is **at the line of scrimmage**. No yardage in the table changed. The lesson text must not describe the hold as downfield.

Nothing else differs. Rows 26 to 32 were added on 2026-10-25 for facts the homepage lessons rely on, read from the same PDF; the only copy change they caused is the "most often" hedge on the safety in lesson 23. The 2026 rule changes (alignment, the 50-yard touchback, onside kicks, disqualification consultation) do not touch the drive.

## Notes for the guides

- **Touchback after a kickoff:** the spot is the 35 for a kick that reaches the end zone without touching the ground in the landing zone, and the 20 if it bounced in the landing zone first. The returner kneeling in the end zone after a kick that landed in the end zone is a 35. A touchback after a punt or a missed field goal from beyond the 20 is the 20 (6-1-5, and Rule 9 for scrimmage kicks, 11-4-2 b).
- **Kickoff format** in 2026: kicking team at the receiving team's 40; receivers in a 5-yard setup zone (their 30 to 35); landing zone from their 20 to the goal line; kicking team cannot move until the ball lands or is touched. Onside kicks can be declared at any time in a game (6-1-6, new in 2026), by telling the Referee before the play clock starts.
- **Field goals and extra points:** the Try kick is from the 15 (a 33-yard kick); a Try ends when either team scores or the ball is dead; a defensive return of a turnover on a Try scores 2 points (11-3-2).
- **Safety:** 2 points, and the team that was scored on kicks to the other team (a free kick from its own 20) (6-1-2 a).
- **Clock:** at the two-minute warning of each half the clock stops. The out-of-bounds restart rule changes inside the two-minute warning of the first half and the last five minutes of the second half (4-3-2 a). The 25-second play clock applies after a change of possession, timeout, the two-minute warning, and a penalty enforcement.
- **Challenges:** coaches have no challenges inside overtime, and none on plays the Replay Official reviews automatically (15-1-1, 16-5-d).
- **Overtime ties:** regular season games can end in a tie after one 10-minute period.
- **Colleges differ:** a catch needs two feet (or another body part) in bounds in the NFL, one foot in college; the book says so in 8-1-3.
- **Penalties worth a guide row:** illegal formation (5), illegal motion, encroachment (5, a defender makes contact before the snap), neutral zone infraction (5), defensive holding (5 and an automatic first down), offensive pass interference (10 from the previous spot), intentional grounding (loss of down plus yardage), unnecessary roughness (15). Rows 35 to 43 now cover each of these.

| 56 | More overtime details | A coin toss starts overtime, and the visiting captain calls it (16-1-2). The receiving team's "opportunity to possess" is the kickoff (16-1-5 c). If the first team scores and the second team then throws an interception or fumbles, the down plays out and any points on it count (16-1-5 a). Only the Replay Official starts reviews in overtime; coaches can't challenge (16-1-5 d). Playoffs: 2-minute breaks between periods, no halftime after the second, teams change ends after the first and third, a new coin toss after the fourth (16-1-4 e, f, i). A defensive score on the first possession: a safety by the kicking team ends the game (16-1-3 a); an interception or fumble returned for a touchdown also ends it, because the defense has then possessed the ball (16-1-5 b) and leads once both teams have had it (16-1-3 b). | rulebook PDF above | for the guides (overtime). | 57 | Who reviews a play, and the standard for changing it | Reviews are conducted by the league's Senior Vice President of Officiating or a designee, in consultation with the Replay Official and the Referee, who watches a field-level monitor (15-2). Once a play is under review, every reviewable part of it can be changed (15-2-2). | rulebook PDF above | for the guides (replay) |
| 58 | What can and can't be reviewed | Reviewable: possession and catches, touching of the ball or the ground, plays at the goal line, the boundary lines, the line of scrimmage and the line to gain, the number of players on the field, game administration (penalty enforcement, the down, the spot of a foul, the clock) and disqualifications (15-3-1). Not reviewable: an erroneous whistle, an illegal bat or kick, intentional grounding, an ineligible receiver downfield, illegal contact on a receiver, an illegal block, and anything not listed (15-4). | 60 | How a challenge is won or lost, and what it costs | A challenge succeeds if any reviewable aspect of the play is changed (15-6-1); an unsuccessful one costs a timeout (15-1-1 e). | 61 | Timeouts | Three charged timeouts per team in each half, and two in a regular-season overtime (4-5-1 Item 1).
